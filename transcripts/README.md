

Better sources, which are critical of power and checkable:
  - Money and banking: the Bank of England's 2014 paper "Money creation in the modern economy"; Werner's own academic papers, which are more
    careful than his interviews; Perry Mehrling; Adam Tooze's Crashed.
  - Asset managers: Benjamin Braun on "asset manager capitalism"; Brett Christophers, Our Lives in Their Portfolios.
  - Who governs: Gilens and Page (Krainer cites them); Thomas Ferguson's Golden Rule, which follows campaign money empirically.
  - Offshore and the City of London: Nicholas Shaxson, Treasure Islands; Oliver Bullough, Butler to the World.
  - Sanctions and chokepoints: Farrell and Newman, Underground Empire, which is Berletic's subject treated by mainstream scholars.
  - War and covert action: Lindsey O'Rourke, Covert Regime Change (Dixon cites it); Craig Whitlock, The Afghanistan Papers; Brown
    University's Costs of War project.
  - National development policy: Ha-Joon Chang, Kicking Away the Ladder, which is Krainer's "American System" argument in scholarly form.
  - Cash and digital money: Brett Scott, Cloudmoney.
