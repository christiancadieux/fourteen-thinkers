Stocks Are for Show, but Bonds Are for Dough

We live under a debt-based monetary regime.  Anchoring that system has been U.S. Treasury bonds.  As we all know, those have been selling off for a while, meaning that rates have been rising, and the U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been very actively attempting to wrestle those rates lower.

Interestingly, that’s really the Federal Reserve’s job, which they must have declined for some reason. I suspect that reason is the Fed is worried about inflation, which consists of two main components: (1) the actual mismatch between money and goods and services and (2) inflation expectations.

It’s that second component that’s the tricky one.  The reality of inflation is one thing, but it’s really our collective expectations of what inflation is going to be in the future that propels the dynamic into hyperdrive.  Example: if inflation is 5% that’s painful but manageable.  If we all think inflation is going to be 10% next year, then we all start spending faster, which makes inflation tick higher.
