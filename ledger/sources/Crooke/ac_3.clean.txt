Is Netanyahu planning to attack Iran and Yemen? (Ibrahim al-Amine, Editor, Al-Akhbar):

-   “The prevailing view is that Israel has no intention of, nor is it capable of opening additional fronts in the region … It was therefore striking that US diplomatic sources warned that Netanyahu’s recent remarks on Lebanon, Syria and Iran are not merely part of the election campaign; that he means what he says; and that he is exerting significant pressure on the security services and the IDF leadership to prepare for a lightning operation against Iran first, and against Hezbollah second.

-   “Sources say that Netanyahu is also offering his services to the Arabian Peninsula states by expressing his willingness to participate in an all-out war against the Houthis in Yemen.

-   “Significant pressure being exerted on him by the US administration is currently focused on the Syrian issue, with the aim of preventing him from expanding the scope of the occupation. It should be noted that there are those in Washington who are hearing a great deal about the Tel Aviv government’s intention to take broader steps to help its Druze allies in Suwayda declare complete secession from Syria.

-   “According to sources, Israeli efforts regarding Iran are centred on an intelligence programme aimed at carrying out a series of strikes and a return to a policy of assassinations targeting political, military and security leaders, through special operations carried out inside Iran, as well as re-establishing contact with separatist Iranian groups to provide them with exceptional support to instigate unrest in various parts of the country, with the ultimate aim of provoking the Iranian leadership into a military confrontation, thereby enabling Israel to launch an air campaign targeting the Iranian state’s infrastructure, particularly the energy, ports and oil production sectors.

-   “The sources express their concern that Netanyahu may be acting on the assumption that his room for manoeuvre is expanding day by day, as a result of the stalling of efforts to reinstate the MOU between Iran and the United States.

-   “In this context, well-informed sources have revealed that Iranian-Omani talks have reached the final stages of drafting a mechanism to regulate navigation through the Strait of Hormuz ... This agreement is viewed as a provisional arrangement for managing maritime traffic in the Strait. Technical and legal teams in Tehran and Muscat have finalised the maps and legal documents relating to the agreement, which are to include a mechanism for the clearance of naval mines. The two sides have also been in contact with the Netherlands with a view to registering the agreement with the International Maritime Organisation and international legal institutions based in The Hague, thereby providing it with legal and international cover.

-   “According to the sources, Iran is proceeding on the basis that Oman is conducting the negotiations in consultation and coordination with the US and regional states; consequently, any agreement accepted by Muscat will, in principle, be acceptable to the other parties. However, Iran has informed the mediators – Pakistan and Oman – that the implementation of the agreement, once signed, will remain contingent upon the US returning to the MOU and considering this solution as addressing the provisions of Clause 5 thereof. This means that the US must lift the naval blockade currently imposed on Iran, remove restrictions on Iranian oil exports, and lift the sanctions imposed on the country. In this scenario, it is assumed that all parties will move on to a new phase aimed at bringing the war to a comprehensive end, including the war in Lebanon.

-   “Whilst sources speak of ‘positive signs’ regarding this issue, they simultaneously point out that developments on the ground over the past two weeks open the door to a serious possibility of the war escalating. They believe that Iran’s harsh responses to US attacks, and the concentrated bombing of US bases in the Gulf and Jordan, are intended to signal to Washington that Tehran is not prepared to make concessions, and that the confrontation could escalate to the point of preventing all maritime traffic in Gulf waters, not just the passage through the Strait of Hormuz.

-   “Agreement on opening the Strait is not the only sticking point in the negotiations. In exchange for resuming the MOU, Iran wants firm guarantees regarding the lifting of the embargo, the release of frozen Iranian funds, and a complete end to the state of war. These conditions require additional elements that may need to be pursued with other parties.

-   “Sources indicate, in this context, that Saudi Arabia is playing a significant role in the behind-the-scenes mediation … Bin Salman has extended an official invitation to the Iranian leadership, amid expectations that an Iranian delegation comprising Qalibaf and FM Araghchi will visit Riyadh in the coming days. The Saudi side wishes to discuss the conflict with Iran and the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, as well as the ongoing war with Ansar Allah. Tehran had previously shown no willingness to engage with proposals concerning Yemen; however, the situation now appears to be different, in light of developments on the ground which have shown Ansar Allah forces making advances across the entire coastal region and taking control of [key] areas, amidst major collapses in the ranks of the forces allied with Saudi Arabia … The UAE, which is standing on the sidelines watching developments … is speaking with schadenfreude about the Saudi role, holding Riyadh responsible for the rift between the forces opposing Ansar Allah.

-   “Sources indicate that the Yemen dossier does not appear to be entirely in Saudi hands, as various indications suggest that the US seems more keen to expand the scope of Saudi involvement in the aggression against the Sana’a government, whilst Washington has resumed bolstering the UAE’s role alongside certain factions in southern Yemen, with the aim of escalating the confrontation with Ansar Allah …

-   “[However], the Bab al-Mandab Strait is now in the hands of Iran and its allied forces, and the battle led by Ansar Allah’s forces is merely part of a broader strategy that will force the US and several other countries to reconsider their calculations. The complete closure of the Bab al-Mandab Strait is no longer merely a theoretical possibility; it has become a card effectively held by Ansar Allah, who have their own calculations and priorities, foremost among which is lifting the blockade on Yemen. Nevertheless, they do not act outside the strategy of the ‘Axis of Resistance’ at the regional level”.
